# Revenue-execution standards & proven practice — deeper notes

Read on demand. For each: what it covers, when to invoke it, and how it is commonly misread.
Cite by name; never invent clause numbers or quotes. This is decision vocabulary, not certification.

## Qualification and forecast evidence

**MEDDPICC (originated at PTC in the 1990s; popularised in *The Qualified Sales Leader*, John McMahon)**
- Covers: Metrics, Economic buyer, Decision criteria, Decision process, Paper process, Identify
  pain, Champion, Competition — the evidence a deal needs to be forecastable.
- Invoke when: deals sit in late stages without an identified economic buyer or paper process, or
  the commit call is challenged.
- Misreading: a form to fill in. Each letter is evidence from the buyer; a rep's belief is not a
  champion.

**Forecast categories (pipeline / best case / commit / closed) and forecast accuracy**
- Covers: categories with explicit entry rules; tracking commit-vs-actual per manager over time.
- Invoke when: the forecast is the sum of rep commits, or misses repeat in the same direction.
- Misreading: changing categories to hit the number. Bias (always over or always under) is the
  signal to fix, not the miss itself.

## Funnel arithmetic and capacity

**Little's law (John Little, 1961)**
- Covers: in a stable system, average work in progress = arrival rate × average time in system.
- Invoke when: cycle time lengthens — the same creation rate then requires more open pipeline,
  and deals slip out of the period.
- Misreading: applying it to an unstable funnel (a launch, a reorg). Use trailing periods that
  share the same motion.

**Ramp-adjusted sales capacity planning**
- Covers: ramped-rep equivalents = Σ (rep × ramp % for their tenure); capacity = equivalents ×
  median productivity × expected attainment; hiring lead time = recruiting time + ramp.
- Invoke when: a target is "solved" by hiring, or attainment is below plan across the team.
- Misreading: using average attainment (skewed by a few stars) or quota as productivity.

**Magic Number (Scale Venture Partners)**
- Covers: net new recurring revenue in a period (annualised) ÷ sales & marketing spend in the
  prior period — a sales-efficiency signal.
- Invoke when: deciding whether to add sales & marketing spend.
- Misreading: one-period readings, or ignoring churn — new ARR that churns inflates the ratio.

**Theory of Constraints applied to the funnel (Goldratt)**
- Covers: the system's output is limited by its tightest step.
- Invoke when: everyone pushes top-of-funnel while the constraint is solution engineering,
  security review or legal turnaround.
- Misreading: assuming the constraint is always lead volume.

## Sales motion and team

**The Sales Acceleration Formula (Mark Roberge, 2015)**
- Covers: data-driven hiring profiles, training and ramp measured by leading indicators, demand
  generation aligned to sales capacity.
- Invoke when: ramp is long or uneven, or hiring outcomes vary widely.
- Misreading: copying another company's hiring scorecard instead of building one from own data.

**Predictable Revenue (Aaron Ross & Marylou Tyler, 2011)**
- Covers: specialising prospecting (SDR/BDR), closing (AE) and account management.
- Invoke when: closers prospect part-time and pipeline creation is erratic.
- Misreading: specialising too early — a small team pays hand-off costs without the volume benefit.

**SPIN Selling (Neil Rackham, 1988) / The Challenger Sale (Matthew Dixon & Brent Adamson, 2011)**
- Covers: question-led discovery for large deals; teaching a commercial insight and taking control
  in consensus buying.
- Invoke when: win rates fall in complex deals or "no decision" is the top loss reason.
- Misreading: treating either as a script; both are about buyer understanding.

**Bow-tie model (Winning by Design)**
- Covers: the customer journey continues past the sale — onboarding, adoption, expansion — measured
  with conversion rates like the acquisition funnel.
- Invoke when: expansion is forecast as a single percentage with no pipeline behind it.
- Misreading: drawing the bow-tie without stage definitions and owners on the right side.

## Incentives and learning

**Win/loss analysis (buyer interviews by a neutral party)**
- Covers: structured interviews with buyers on won and lost deals; consistent reason coding.
- Invoke when: loss reasons are entered by reps ("price") and never verified.
- Misreading: analysing losses only — wins show which value actually decided the deal.

**Goodhart's law (1975) & Campbell's law (1976)**
- Covers: a measure that becomes a target stops measuring well.
- Invoke when: designing quota and comp — bookings-only pay drives bad-fit deals and
  end-of-quarter discounting.
- Misreading: dropping targets. Pair the target with guardrails (discount level, retention, margin).
