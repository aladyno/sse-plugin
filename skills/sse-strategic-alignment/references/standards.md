# Strategy deployment & alignment standards — deeper notes

Read on demand. For each: what it covers, when to invoke it, and how it is commonly misread.
Cite by name; never invent clause numbers or quotes. This is decision vocabulary, not certification.

## Structuring the cascade

**OKRs (Andy Grove, *High Output Management*, 1983; John Doerr, *Measure What Matters*, 2018)**
- Covers: a few qualitative objectives, each with measurable key results; alignment through
  transparency (everyone's OKRs visible) and a mix of top-down and bottom-up goals; regular check-ins.
- Invoke when: the goal tree is being drawn or repaired, and a team goal must name the company goal
  it serves.
- Misreading: a rigid top-down cascade where each level copies its parent's key results as its own
  objective; or key results that are task lists. Doerr himself warns against purely cascaded OKRs —
  links matter, identical wording doesn't.

**Hoshin Kanri / policy deployment (Yoji Akao, ed., *Hoshin Kanri: Policy Deployment for Successful
TQM*, 1991)**
- Covers: a few breakthrough objectives deployed level by level through catchball — each level
  proposes its contribution, the level above negotiates, until plans connect; the X-matrix relates
  long-term objectives, annual objectives, improvement priorities, measures and owners; periodic
  reviews of deployment.
- Invoke when: repairing a broken cascade — gaps and conflicts are resolved by negotiation between
  levels, not by memo.
- Misreading: catchball as a one-way briefing with a feedback form, or the X-matrix as the
  deliverable. The negotiation and the owner per priority are the point.

**Balanced Scorecard & strategy maps (Robert Kaplan & David Norton, *The Balanced Scorecard*, 1996;
*Strategy Maps*, 2004; *Alignment*, 2006)**
- Covers: objectives across financial, customer, internal-process and learning-and-growth
  perspectives, linked by cause and effect; *Alignment* extends this to business units and to
  support units (HR, IT, finance) aligning with the units they serve.
- Invoke when: a link is "theoretical" — ask for the causal chain from the team's objective to the
  company outcome; or when support functions set goals in isolation.
- Misreading: a scorecard as a dashboard of unrelated KPIs per perspective. Without the causal
  arrows it isn't a strategy map.

## Closing the gap between strategy and operations

**The Execution Premium (Kaplan & Norton, 2008)**
- Covers: a closed-loop management system — develop and translate strategy, align the
  organisation, plan operations, monitor and learn, test and adapt; separate strategy review
  meetings from operational review meetings; link budgets and incentives to strategic objectives.
- Invoke when: strategy is reviewed only inside operational meetings, or budgets and incentives
  were never changed after the strategy was.
- Misreading: adopting the full office-of-strategy-management apparatus in a small company; the
  usable core is the separate strategy review and the budget/incentive link.

**The Art of Action (Stephen Bungay, 2011)**
- Covers: three gaps between plans, actions and outcomes — knowledge, alignment and effects —
  addressed by directed opportunism: brief intent (what and why), let people decide how, and ask
  for a back-brief to check what was understood.
- Invoke when: diagnosing a communication gap — run a back-brief rather than another announcement.
- Misreading: "give people autonomy" without stating intent and boundaries first; autonomy without
  intent widens the alignment gap.

**The 4 Disciplines of Execution (Chris McChesney, Sean Covey & Jim Huling, 2012)**
- Covers: focus on one or two wildly important goals; act on lead measures; keep a compelling
  scoreboard; create a cadence of accountability.
- Invoke when: teams carry so many goals that the strategic one is lost in day-to-day work.
- Misreading: lead measures that are just activity counts with no tested link to the lag measure.

## Structure, seams and silos

**Team Topologies (Matthew Skelton & Manuel Pais, 2019)**
- Covers: four team types (stream-aligned, platform, enabling, complicated-subsystem), three
  interaction modes (collaboration, X-as-a-Service, facilitating), cognitive load as a design
  constraint.
- Invoke when: a seam has no owner, or dependencies between teams are implicit and keep blocking.
  Name the interaction mode each seam should have.
- Misreading: renaming existing teams to the four types without changing responsibilities or
  interactions.

**Conway's law (Melvin Conway, "How Do Committees Invent?", 1968) & the inverse Conway maneuver**
- Covers: systems mirror the communication structure of the organisation that builds them; the
  inverse maneuver shapes teams to the architecture or outcome wanted.
- Invoke when: the strategy needs an outcome that cuts across the current team boundaries.
- Misreading: reorganising as a substitute for deciding goal ownership; structure follows the
  strategy, not the reverse.

**Team of Teams (Gen. Stanley McChrystal et al., 2015)**
- Covers: at scale, efficiency-optimised silos fail in complex environments; shared consciousness
  (broad transparency, cross-team forums, liaisons) plus empowered execution.
- Invoke when: teams don't know what adjacent teams are doing and coordination only flows upward.
- Misreading: more meetings as "shared consciousness"; the test is whether teams can describe
  each other's priorities.

**The Five Dysfunctions of a Team (Patrick Lencioni, 2002)**
- Covers: absence of trust, fear of conflict, lack of commitment, avoidance of accountability,
  inattention to results.
- Invoke when: the leadership team itself pulls apart — leaders defend their department's goals
  over the leadership team's collective results.
- Misreading: treating it as a team-building exercise; the alignment fix is a shared result the
  leadership team is measured on together.

## Incentives, change and oversight

**Goodhart's law (Charles Goodhart, 1975) & Campbell's law (Donald Campbell, 1976)**
- Covers: a measure that becomes a target stops measuring well; quantitative indicators used for
  decisions get distorted.
- Invoke when: a team's incentive metric conflicts with a company goal; pair it with a guardrail
  metric owned jointly with the affected team.
- Misreading: dropping measurement altogether; the remedy is paired measures and judgement.

**Kotter's 8-step change model (John Kotter, *Leading Change*, 1996)**
- Covers: urgency, guiding coalition, vision, communication, removing obstacles, short-term wins,
  consolidating gains, anchoring in culture.
- Invoke when: re-cascading after a strategy change.
- Misreading: stopping at "communicate the vision" while systems, incentives and budgets still
  reward the old priorities.

**ISO 37000:2021 — Governance of organizations (guidance)**
- Covers: the governing body's responsibilities, including directing strategy and overseeing its
  implementation, accountability and stakeholder engagement.
- Invoke when: a board asks how it can see whether strategy is deployed, not just approved.
- Misreading: treating it as a certifiable management system; it is guidance for governing bodies.
