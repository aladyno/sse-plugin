# Strategy, governance & decision standards — deeper notes

Read on demand. For each: what it covers, when to invoke it, and how it is commonly misread.
Cite by name; never invent clause numbers or quotes. This is decision vocabulary, not certification.

## Forming and testing a direction

**Strategy kernel (Richard Rumelt, *Good Strategy/Bad Strategy*, 2011)**
- Covers: good strategy = diagnosis (what is going on, the crux) + guiding policy (the approach to
  the crux) + coherent actions (coordinated, resourced steps). Names the hallmarks of bad strategy:
  fluff, failure to face the challenge, mistaking goals for strategy, bad strategic objectives.
- Invoke when: a plan is a list of goals or initiatives with no stated challenge; or every function
  has its own priorities that don't reinforce each other.
- Misreading: treating the kernel as a document template. A diagnosis without evidence or a policy
  that excludes nothing passes the template and still fails the test.

**Playing to Win (A.G. Lafley & Roger Martin, 2013)**
- Covers: a cascade of five linked choices — winning aspiration, where to play, how to win, required
  capabilities, management systems.
- Invoke when: the choice itself is unclear — which segments, channels, geographies, and on what
  basis (cost or differentiation) the company intends to win.
- Misreading: answering each box separately. The choices must fit together; "where to play"
  without "where not to play" is not a choice.

**Jobs to be Done & disruption theory (Clayton Christensen, *The Innovator's Dilemma*, 1997;
*Competing Against Luck*, 2016)**
- Covers: customers "hire" products to make progress in a circumstance; incumbents are displaced by
  entrants who start at the low end or in a new market the incumbent rationally ignores.
- Invoke when: diagnosing demand (why customers buy or churn) and when a cheaper, "worse" competitor
  is dismissed.
- Misreading: calling every new competitor "disruptive", or every feature request a "job".

**Wardley Mapping (Simon Wardley)**
- Covers: a value chain plotted against evolution (genesis → custom-built → product → commodity);
  movement and gameplay on the map.
- Invoke when: deciding where to differentiate, what to buy or outsource, and where a market is
  about to commoditise.
- Misreading: drawing the map as an end in itself; one question usually suffices — is the thing we
  invest in still differentiating, or already commodity?

## Allocating across bets

**Three Horizons (Mehrdad Baghai, Stephen Coley & David White, *The Alchemy of Growth*, 1999)**
- Covers: managing the core business (H1), emerging businesses (H2) and options for the future (H3)
  simultaneously, each with different metrics and management style.
- Invoke when: checking whether the portfolio of bets is balanced, and whether new bets are judged
  by core-business metrics (which kills them early).
- Misreading: copying a fixed percentage split from an article. The split follows the diagnosis,
  cash horizon and evidence per bet; name the current and intended split instead.

**OKRs (Andy Grove, *High Output Management*; John Doerr, *Measure What Matters*, 2018)**
- Covers: objectives (qualitative direction) with a few measurable key results; cascading alignment
  and regular check-ins.
- Invoke when: translating agreed bets into outcomes teams can steer by.
- Misreading: OKRs as the strategy, or key results that are task lists. OKRs express a strategy; they
  don't replace the diagnosis.

## Decision quality under uncertainty

**One-way vs two-way doors (Jeff Bezos, Amazon 2015 shareholder letter)**
- Covers: Type 1 decisions (consequential, near irreversible) deserve slow, careful process; Type 2
  (reversible) should be made quickly by small groups. The 2016 letter adds "disagree and commit".
- Invoke when: setting how much evidence and who must be involved before deciding a bet.
- Misreading: labelling a decision two-way because the product can be changed back, while hires,
  contracts, public commitments or customer trust cannot.

**Thinking in Bets (2018) and Quit (2022) (Annie Duke)**
- Covers: separating decision quality from outcome ("resulting"); pre-committing to kill criteria as
  states and dates; sunk cost and escalation of commitment.
- Invoke when: a bet is under review after a good or bad outcome; before funding any new bet.
- Misreading: setting the criteria after results arrive, or moving the date when it is missed.

**Outside view (Daniel Kahneman & Dan Lovallo, "Timid Choices and Bold Forecasts", 1993) &
premortem (Gary Klein, HBR 2007)**
- Covers: forecasts anchored on the inside view are systematically optimistic; start from a
  reference class of similar ventures. Premortem: assume the bet failed and explain why.
- Invoke when: a plan's timeline or adoption curve rests only on the team's own estimate.
- Misreading: picking a flattering reference class, or running the premortem after commitment.

## Governance, risk and stakeholders

**ISO 37000:2021 — Governance of organizations (guidance)**
- Covers: principles for governing bodies — purpose, value generation, strategy, oversight,
  accountability, stakeholder engagement, leadership, data and decisions, risk governance, social
  responsibility, viability and performance over time.
- Invoke when: framing a bet for a board — how it serves purpose, who is accountable, how it will
  be overseen.
- Misreading: treating it as a certifiable management system; it is guidance for governing bodies.

**G20/OECD Principles of Corporate Governance (revised 2023)**
- Covers: shareholder rights and equitable treatment, the role of institutional investors,
  disclosure and transparency, responsibilities of the board, sustainability and resilience.
- Invoke when: board composition, information to shareholders, or selective disclosure is at stake.
- Misreading: assuming they apply only to listed companies; the disclosure and board-duty logic is
  useful to any company with outside investors. Local company and securities law prevails.

**ISO 31000 (risk management) & COSO ERM — *Integrating with Strategy and Performance* (2017)**
- Covers: risk as the effect of uncertainty on objectives; COSO ties risk appetite to strategy
  selection and performance.
- Invoke when: a bet is argued with adjectives ("risky", "safe") — restate as which objective is
  threatened, how likely, who bears it, and the treatment chosen.
- Misreading: a risk register as the deliverable; the decision, its owner and appetite are.

**Kotter's 8-step change model (John Kotter, *Leading Change*, 1996)**
- Covers: urgency, guiding coalition, vision, communication, removing obstacles, short-term wins,
  consolidating gains, anchoring in culture.
- Invoke when: a new direction must move people, not just budgets.
- Misreading: running the steps as a communications campaign while systems (incentives, hiring,
  budgets) still reward the old behaviour.
