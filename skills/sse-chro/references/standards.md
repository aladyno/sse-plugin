# People-strategy standards — deeper notes

Read on demand. For each: what it covers, when to invoke it, and how it is commonly misread.
Cite by name; never invent clause numbers or quotes. This is decision vocabulary, not legal or
HR certification.

## Measuring the workforce

**ISO 30414:2018 (Human resource management — guidelines for internal and external human capital reporting)**
- Covers: recommended human-capital metrics across areas such as costs, productivity, recruitment
  and turnover, leadership, succession, skills, diversity, health and safety, compliance.
- Invoke when: deciding which people metrics leadership or a board should see.
- Misreading: reporting all of them. Pick the few tied to the plan's risks and keep definitions stable.

**Regrettable vs non-regrettable attrition (industry practice)**
- Covers: separating voluntary exits the company wanted to avoid from exits it accepts or initiates.
- Invoke when: diagnosing retention; sizing the "− exits" cell of the headcount bridge.
- Misreading: classifying after the fact to make numbers look better. Decide the criteria in advance
  (e.g. rating ≥ "meets" and in a critical role).

**eNPS (Employee Net Promoter Score) · Gallup Q12**
- Covers: one-question advocacy signal; Q12 = twelve engagement items linked in Gallup research to
  outcomes such as turnover and productivity.
- Invoke when: a trend signal per team/manager is needed.
- Misreading: one company-wide score as a KPI target. Value lies in trend and in manager-level cuts,
  and in acting on results quickly.

## Hiring

**ISO 30405:2016 (Human resource management — guidelines on recruitment)**
- Covers: recruitment process — planning, sourcing, assessment, offer, candidate experience.
- Invoke when: designing a repeatable, fair hiring process across teams or countries.
- Misreading: process for its own sake — the outcome measures are time-to-fill, quality of hire and
  offer acceptance.

**Scorecard method (Geoff Smart & Randy Street, *Who*, 2008)**
- Covers: per-role mission, 3–8 measurable outcomes, and competencies, written before sourcing.
- Invoke when: a role has no clear definition of success; interviewers disagree.
- Misreading: a job description renamed. A scorecard states outcomes with numbers and dates.

**Structured interviews (Schmidt & Hunter, 1998 meta-analysis; later updates)**
- Covers: evidence that structured interviews and work samples predict job performance better than
  unstructured interviews.
- Invoke when: interview loops rely on "gut feel" or vary by interviewer.
- Misreading: structure = rigid script; it means consistent questions + a rubric + independent scoring.

## Levels, pay and equity

**Job architecture / leveling (market practice; e.g. Radford/Aon, Mercer IPE)**
- Covers: job families × levels with defined scope, impact and expectations; bands attached to levels.
- Invoke when: building ladders, setting bands, settling promotion criteria.
- Misreading: title inflation to win candidates; it breaks bands and the ladder for everyone after.

**Compa-ratio and percentile positioning**
- Covers: salary ÷ band midpoint; target market percentile per role family.
- Invoke when: auditing offers and the existing team for under/over-payment and compression.
- Misreading: one percentile for every role. Scarce, critical roles may justify a higher target —
  state it explicitly.

**EU Pay Transparency Directive (EU) 2023/970**
- Covers: pay-range disclosure to candidates, ban on asking salary history, gender pay-gap reporting
  (phased by employer size), joint pay assessment where an unexplained gap of 5% or more persists.
  Member-state transposition deadline: June 2026.
- Invoke when: the company employs people in the EU, or wants a defensible pay-equity practice anywhere.
- Misreading: "only applies to large companies" — several obligations apply regardless of size;
  exact obligations by country are a question for counsel (`sse-gc`).

**Equity vesting norms (4-year vesting, 1-year cliff; refresh grants)**
- Covers: common startup vesting schedule; refreshes keep unvested value for retained people.
- Invoke when: mapping retention-risk dates for key people.
- Misreading: refreshing only executives; high performers at full vest are the predictable exits.

## Organisation design and development

**Span of control and layers (organisation-design practice)**
- Covers: manager capacity (often ~6–10 direct ICs; fewer for managers of managers), number of layers.
- Invoke when: the org grows past ~30 people, or decisions bottleneck at one person.
- Misreading: adding managers for title reasons; layers should follow span breakage.

**Kirkpatrick model (Donald Kirkpatrick; four levels: reaction, learning, behaviour, results)**
- Covers: evaluating training and onboarding.
- Invoke when: justifying onboarding or L&D spend.
- Misreading: stopping at level 1 (satisfaction). Measure ramp time and behaviour change.

**Local labour law (e.g. Vietnam Labour Code 2019 — Law No. 45/2019/QH14)**
- Covers: contract types, probation, working hours, termination and notice rules per jurisdiction.
- Invoke when: plans span countries or change contract types/termination practice.
- Misreading: assuming one policy fits all locations. Obligations are legal questions → counsel.
