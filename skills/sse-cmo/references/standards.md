# Marketing & positioning standards — deeper notes

Read on demand. For each: what it covers, when to invoke it, and how it is commonly misread.
Cite by name; never invent clause numbers or quotes. This is decision vocabulary, not certification.

## Positioning and the job

**Obviously Awesome (April Dunford, 2019)**
- Covers: a repeatable positioning process — competitive alternatives → unique attributes → value
  those attributes enable → customers who care most → market category that makes the value obvious.
- Invoke when: filling the **Position** link, or when the message differs between website, deck
  and sales calls.
- Misreading: treating positioning as a tagline exercise. It is a decision about context; the
  tagline comes last and is marketing's job, not the positioning itself.

**Jobs To Be Done (Clayton Christensen; Tony Ulwick's Outcome-Driven Innovation; Bob Moesta)**
- Covers: customers "hire" a product to make progress in a specific circumstance; the competitor is
  whatever they hire today, including doing nothing.
- Invoke when: filling the **Job & alternative** link; when "we have no competition" is claimed.
- Misreading: writing jobs as features ("needs a dashboard"). A job is progress in the customer's
  words, independent of any solution.

**Crossing the Chasm (Geoffrey Moore)**
- Covers: the gap between early adopters and the pragmatic majority in B2B technology; win a
  beachhead segment completely, then expand to adjacent ones.
- Invoke when: the ICP is broad and early, sales cycles are inconsistent, references don't transfer.
- Misreading: picking a beachhead by market size. Pick the segment with the most compelling reason
  to buy and the strongest word-of-mouth between its members.

**Category Design (*Play Bigger*, Ramadan, Peterson, Lochhead, Maney)**
- Covers: creating and naming a new category so the company defines the problem it then solves.
- Invoke when: the product genuinely doesn't fit an existing category buyers search for.
- Misreading: declaring a category as a campaign. It needs years and leadership commitment; most
  products are better positioned inside an existing category with a sharp sub-segment.

## Brand and demand

**How Brands Grow (Byron Sharp, Ehrenberg-Bass Institute)**
- Covers: empirical laws of buying — growth mostly from light/occasional buyers, mental and physical
  availability, distinctive brand assets, double jeopardy.
- Invoke when: debating niche targeting vs. reach; when loyalty programmes are proposed as the
  growth plan. Strongest for consumer and high-volume markets.
- Misreading: "never target". Sharp argues against over-narrow targeting of media, not against a
  clear ICP in B2B sales.

**The Long and the Short of It (Les Binet & Peter Field, IPA)**
- Covers: brand building drives long-term growth and pricing power; activation drives short-term
  sales; both are needed, on different clocks.
- Invoke when: brand spend is judged on next-month leads, or performance spend takes everything.
- Misreading: quoting a fixed brand/activation split as universal. The ratios they report vary by
  category and context; use them as a starting hypothesis.

## Growth motions and channels

**Product-Led Growth (Wes Bush; OpenView)**
- Covers: the product as the primary acquisition, conversion and expansion channel.
- Invoke when: choosing the primary motion; check time-to-value, ACV and whether the user can buy.
- Misreading: "add a free tier" = PLG. Without a fast path to value and self-serve buying it only
  adds support cost.

**Growth loops (Reforge)**
- Covers: compounding systems where each cycle's output becomes the next cycle's input
  (user-generated content, referral, paid-reinvested revenue).
- Invoke when: growth stalls despite more spend in a linear funnel.
- Misreading: drawing loops that don't close — if the output doesn't measurably feed the input, it
  is a funnel.

**Traction — Bullseye framework (Gabriel Weinberg & Justin Mares)**
- Covers: brainstorm many channels, test a few cheaply, focus on the one that works.
- Invoke when: five channels at equal weight; nobody knows which one pays.
- Misreading: endless testing. The point is to concentrate once one channel shows payback.

**SaaS unit economics — LTV:CAC and CAC payback (public benchmark reports, e.g. Bessemer, KeyBanc)**
- Covers: whether acquisition spend returns; commonly cited healthy zones are LTV:CAC around or above
  3:1 and payback within 12–18 months, varying by stage and ACV.
- Invoke when: filling the **Payback** link.
- Misreading: blended numbers; LTV computed from revenue rather than gross margin; benchmarks taken
  as targets for a business with a different model.

## Measurement and compliance

**ISO 20671 (brand evaluation — principles and fundamentals)**
- Covers: vocabulary and principles for evaluating brand strength through inputs, behaviours and
  outcomes.
- Invoke when: someone needs a defensible way to talk about brand value to leadership.
- Misreading: expecting a formula; it frames evaluation, it doesn't produce a number.

**ICC Advertising and Marketing Communications Code; GDPR / ePrivacy (EU) and local equivalents**
- Covers: truthful, substantiated claims; comparative advertising rules; lawful basis and consent
  for tracking cookies and marketing email.
- Invoke when: comparative claims against named rivals; new tracking, list buying or retargeting.
- Misreading: assuming B2B email is exempt everywhere. Rules differ by jurisdiction — route to
  `sse-gc` for the legal call.
