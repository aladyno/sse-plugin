# Commercial standards & proven practice — deeper notes

Read on demand. For each: what it covers, when to invoke it, and how it is commonly misread.
Cite by name; never invent clause numbers or quotes. This is decision vocabulary, not certification.

## Definitions and unit economics

**IFRS 15 / ASC 606 — Revenue from Contracts with Customers (IASB / FASB, converged standard)**
- Covers: the five-step model — identify the contract, identify performance obligations, determine
  the transaction price, allocate it, recognise revenue when (or as) obligations are satisfied.
- Invoke when: bookings, billings, recognised revenue and run-rate (ARR/MRR, GMV) are being mixed in
  one number, or a board figure doesn't reconcile with finance.
- Misreading: treating ARR, GMV or bookings as accounting revenue. They are management measures;
  define each once and reconcile it to recognised revenue. Recognition questions → finance/auditors.

**SaaS metrics (David Skok, "SaaS Metrics 2.0", For Entrepreneurs blog)**
- Covers: CAC and CAC payback, LTV:CAC, gross and net revenue retention, cohort analysis, the
  "SaaS cash flow trough".
- Invoke when: deciding whether to spend more on acquisition, or comparing segments and channels.
- Misreading: blended CAC across all channels and segments, or LTV computed from a churn rate too
  young to be stable. Compute by segment and channel; prefer payback (observable) over LTV (modelled).
  For transactional models, apply the same logic to contribution per customer instead of ARR.

## Pricing and packaging

**The Strategy and Tactics of Pricing (Thomas Nagle & Georg Müller; earlier eds. with Reed Holden)**
- Covers: value-based pricing from economic value to the customer; price structure (metric, fences,
  bundles); price realisation and the policy that stops discount leakage.
- Invoke when: price is set as cost-plus or competitor-minus; discounts negotiated deal by deal.
- Misreading: "value-based" as charging whatever the market bears. It starts with quantifying the
  customer's outcome against their next-best alternative.

**Monetizing Innovation (Madhavan Ramanujam & Georg Tacke, 2016)**
- Covers: discussing willingness to pay before building; segmenting by willingness to pay;
  good/better/best packaging; leaders, fillers and killers among features.
- Invoke when: designing tiers, launching a new offer, or a tier exists that nobody buys.
- Misreading: three tiers as a template. Tiers follow segments with different willingness to pay.

**Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Meter / Gabor-Granger / conjoint analysis**
- Covers: survey methods for acceptable price ranges (Van Westendorp), demand at given prices
  (Gabor-Granger), and trade-offs between features and price (conjoint).
- Invoke when: there is no price history for a new offer, or before a price change.
- Misreading: survey answers as demand. Stated intent is a hypothesis; validate with win rate,
  conversion and churn after a controlled rollout.

## Selling and forecasting

**MEDDPICC (evolved from MEDDIC, originated at PTC in the 1990s)**
- Covers: Metrics, Economic buyer, Decision criteria, Decision process, Paper process, Identify
  pain, Champion, Competition.
- Invoke when: deciding what counts as a forecastable deal, or defining stage exit criteria by buyer
  evidence.
- Misreading: a form reps fill in after the fact. Its value is as the evidence a deal needs to move
  stage and enter the forecast.

**SPIN Selling (Neil Rackham, 1988) / The Challenger Sale (Matthew Dixon & Brent Adamson, 2011)**
- Covers: SPIN — situation, problem, implication, need-payoff questions, from research on large
  sales; Challenger — teach, tailor, take control, for consensus-driven buying.
- Invoke when: deals stall at discovery, or win rate drops in complex multi-stakeholder sales.
- Misreading: Challenger as "be aggressive". It is about bringing a commercial insight the buyer
  didn't have.

**Predictable Revenue (Aaron Ross & Marylou Tyler, 2011) / The Sales Acceleration Formula (Mark
Roberge, 2015)**
- Covers: specialising roles (prospecting vs. closing vs. account management); hiring, training,
  coaching and demand generation driven by measured data.
- Invoke when: planning sales capacity or hiring from a revenue target.
- Misreading: specialisation at any scale. Splitting roles adds hand-offs; it pays only when volume
  justifies it.

## Market, retention and incentives

**Crossing the Chasm (Geoffrey Moore, 1991; rev. 2014)**
- Covers: the gap between early adopters and the early majority; winning a beachhead segment with a
  whole product before expanding.
- Invoke when: the ICP is "everyone", or wins are spread thinly across unrelated segments.
- Misreading: a label for any slow growth. Use it for segment focus, not as a diagnosis by default.

**Customer Success (Nick Mehta, Dan Steinman & Lincoln Murphy, 2016)**
- Covers: recurring revenue depends on customers reaching their desired outcome; health measured from
  behaviour; customer success as a company-wide responsibility.
- Invoke when: churn is handled only at renewal time, or health is a gut-feel rating.
- Misreading: a health score as a vanity dashboard. Its weights must be tested against actual churn.

**Goodhart's law (Charles Goodhart, 1975) & Campbell's law (Donald Campbell, 1976)**
- Covers: a measure that becomes a target stops measuring well; indicators used for decisions get
  distorted.
- Invoke when: designing quotas and comp plans — bookings-only pay produces bad-fit deals and
  end-of-period discounting.
- Misreading: dropping targets. Pair each target with a guardrail (retention, margin, discount level).

**ISO 10002:2018 — Customer satisfaction: guidelines for complaints handling in organizations**
- Covers: a process to receive, track, respond to and learn from complaints.
- Invoke when: churn reasons are unknown because complaints are not captured or analysed.
- Misreading: treating it as a certifiable requirement; it is guidance. The usable core is that
  complaints are recorded, categorised and fed back to product and sales.
