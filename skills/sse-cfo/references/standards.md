# Finance standards & proven practice — deeper notes

Read on demand. For each: what it covers, when to invoke it, and how it is commonly misread.
Cite by name; never invent clause numbers or quotes. This is decision vocabulary, not certification
or accounting advice — treatment questions go to qualified accountants and auditors.

## Cash and definitions

**IAS 7 — Statement of Cash Flows (IASB)**
- Covers: classifying cash flows into operating, investing and financing activities; reconciling
  profit to operating cash flow.
- Invoke when: runway is argued from the P&L, or a profitable month still drains cash (annual
  prepayments, receivables delays, capitalised costs, debt repayments).
- Misreading: "EBITDA-positive means we're safe". Runway is a cash measure; build it from the cash
  flow, not from profit.

**IFRS 15 / ASC 606 — Revenue from Contracts with Customers (IASB / FASB)**
- Covers: the five-step model for recognising revenue as performance obligations are satisfied.
- Invoke when: bookings, billings, recognised revenue and ARR/MRR are mixed in one plan or deck.
- Misreading: treating ARR as revenue. ARR is a management measure; define it once and reconcile.

## Survival and efficiency

**"Default Alive or Default Dead?" (Paul Graham, essay, 2015)**
- Covers: on current expenses and revenue growth, does the company reach profitability with the
  cash it has?
- Invoke when: a plan assumes the next round. Test the plan with no new capital first.
- Misreading: assuming growth rates stay constant. Use the bear-case growth, and re-run monthly.

**Burn multiple (David Sacks, Craft Ventures, 2020)**
- Covers: net burn ÷ net new ARR in the same period — how much cash each unit of new recurring
  revenue costs.
- Invoke when: judging whether spend buys durable growth, or comparing periods.
- Misreading: one quarter's reading as a verdict; lumpy deals distort it. Use a trailing trend and
  pair it with gross retention (new ARR that churns is not durable).

**Rule of 40 (popularised by Brad Feld, 2015)**
- Covers: revenue growth rate + profit margin (often EBITDA or free-cash-flow margin) ≥ 40%.
- Invoke when: a scaled company trades growth against profitability.
- Misreading: applying it to early-stage companies, or mixing margin definitions across periods.

**SaaS metrics (David Skok, "SaaS Metrics 2.0", For Entrepreneurs blog)**
- Covers: CAC, CAC payback, LTV:CAC, cohort retention, the cash-flow trough of fast growth.
- Invoke when: deciding to scale a channel, or explaining why growth consumes cash.
- Misreading: blended CAC and LTV; LTV from an immature churn rate. Prefer payback by channel and
  cohort — it is observable; LTV is modelled.

## Planning and allocation

**Beyond Budgeting (Jeremy Hope & Robin Fraser, *Beyond Budgeting*, 2003; BBRT)**
- Covers: rolling forecasts driven by operational drivers; separating targets, forecasts and
  resource allocation; dynamic funding instead of annual fixed budgets.
- Invoke when: the annual budget is stale by Q2, or forecasts are padded because they double as targets.
- Misreading: "no budgets means no control". Control moves to driver-based forecasts, thresholds
  and fast re-allocation.

**Opportunity cost, hurdle rate and NPV (Brealey, Myers & Allen, *Principles of Corporate Finance*)**
- Covers: a spend is worth it only if its return beats the required return of the next-best use;
  discounting future cash flows.
- Invoke when: competing uses of the same cash (hiring vs. marketing vs. product), or build vs. buy.
- Misreading: precise NPVs from guessed inputs. Use ranges and sensitivity; the ranking matters more
  than the decimal.

**Zero-based budgeting (Peter Pyhrr, 1970s)**
- Covers: justifying each cost line from zero rather than from last year's base.
- Invoke when: cost lines grew by inertia and a cut is needed.
- Misreading: running it every year for every line — costly; use it for targeted reviews.

## Capital and controls

**Venture financing — *Venture Deals* (Brad Feld & Jason Mendelson); NVCA model legal documents;
Y Combinator post-money SAFE**
- Covers: term-sheet economics (price, liquidation preference, anti-dilution, option pool) and control
  (board composition, protective provisions, drag-along); standard document templates.
- Invoke when: comparing offers, modelling dilution, or deciding raise size and timing.
- Misreading: optimising headline valuation while accepting participating preference, a large
  pre-money pool top-up, or control terms that cost more than the price gained. Legal review → counsel.

**COSO Internal Control — Integrated Framework (2013)**
- Covers: control environment, risk assessment, control activities, information and
  communication, monitoring.
- Invoke when: finance grows past one person — approval limits, segregation of duties, monthly
  reconciliations, fraud risk.
- Misreading: a documentation exercise. The test is whether a wrong payment or a misstatement
  would be caught, by whom, and how fast.

**ISO 31000 — Risk management guidelines**
- Covers: risk as consequence × likelihood with a chosen treatment (avoid, reduce, share, retain).
- Invoke when: currency, concentration, counterparty or covenant risk is described with adjectives.
- Misreading: a risk register as the deliverable; the decision, its owner and its trigger are.
